📖 Overview

Cultural Globalization, is described as the standardization of culture, and the experience we share, through the merge . (Watson) The interconnectedness of the world, is theorised to have made cultures less prominent, even to the point of talking about a global culture.

Whilst the global culture is still a theory, anthropologists have now taken out the knowledge system—that is inherited from the ancestors —as part of the definition of a culture, to switch it out for:" ideas and expectations influencing everyday life". (Watson)

On the contrary, the use of "Chrono-urbanism " has risen in the past couple of years, as a critique of modern city culture. (Antunes , Claudia , et al.) Most of the modern cities have had an enormous change in urbanization, where the navigation of the city is often 15-20 minutes. (Antunes , Claudia , et al.)

This is a problem, because car focused city planning can disrupt the lives of millions, due to air pollution . In fact, studies show, that in 2012 around 7 million deaths were the result of city pollution, as a direct result of cars. (World Health Organization)

⚡ Its Impact

The biggest impact of cultural globalization , on local cultures , is that it makes cultures more noticeable on a global scale. (Addis, Ginevra.) However, cultural globalization, simplifies the culture into a few distinct parts, in the eye of the public. (Addis, Ginevra.) This effect results in the change of solving problems at national level. At national level, some problems become too small whilst others become too big for a nation to solve. (Addis, Ginevra.) This homogeneity results in improved efficiency in problem management, but also the simplification of problem solving. (Addis, Ginevra.)

Cultural globalization has also become a magnifier for individuals as well. Through TV, and other media consumption services, companies share their messages to the biggest audience they can.(Kaul, Vineet.) This is done through flexible pricing. A TV show in America is shown to have a higher price in selling than in any third world country. .(Kaul, Vineet.) This benefits the spread of the message, because a company can balance out the production price, with the price that countries can afford that product, to make it sustainable to show to the larger public

🔧 Problems & Solutions

A major problem with cultural globalisation, is the dangers uncontrolled tourism causes.

On the base, tourist vandalism does not arise from pure wrongdoing or boredom, rather from not knowing the consequence of their own actions.

(Madani, Javad, et al.)

For example, it is a frequent phenomenon in Toulourenc Gorges, France, visitors create artificial ponds, which disrupts oxygen in the region.

(University of Luxembourg,) One or two of these hiccups wouldn't be a problem, however it has become a far too big destination, with about 51,000 to 115,000 visitors annually, to easily stop the problem. As a result, it is not just the local fish population disruption that happens, but also now research indicates, that fecal bacteria has risen every summer from 2022 to 2025. (Ministère Chargé de La Santé) (University of Luxembourg,)

This is why education about the local culture is important. Studies show significant improvements from vandalism, in particular when the tourist or the group tested is educated about the problem. (Barker, Mary)

In the end, the problems and solutions in cultural globalization really come down to these key factors: the amount of tourists and the educators willing to teach these groups. When we educate the people, we are preventing vandalism, from growing in the tourist minds. When it is saved at large scale, the outliers become unnoticeable and the new norm becomes the baseness—in a good scenario, the preservation of cultural heritage, with just a few negative outliers.
